## Meter vs Fixed Price Taxi in Lebanon: Which Is Better?

Many first-time visitors ask whether Lebanese taxis use a meter. In practice, most taxis in Lebanon **don't rely on a working meter**. The price is usually agreed verbally. That can work out fine, but it's where most misunderstandings begin.

### Option 1: Verbal price from a street taxi
- **Pros:** You can find one quickly in busy areas like Hamra, Downtown or Mar Mikhael.
- **Cons:** The price depends on your negotiation, your accent and how the driver reads you. The fare can change on arrival, with "extra" charges for bags, tolls or waiting.

### Option 2: Ride-hailing app price
- **Pros:** You see an upfront price and a digital record of the trip.
- **Cons:** Prices rise sharply during peak hours, at night and in bad weather. Local travellers report fares jumping within minutes. Some drivers also cancel after accepting, or ask for more than the app showed.

### Option 3: Pre-booked fixed price on WhatsApp (Andrews Taxi)

- **Cons:** You need to book ahead. For same-day rides, a short WhatsApp message is usually enough.

For most visitors, a **fixed price** is the clear winner. You know your budget before you leave the airport or hotel.

## Service vs Taxi in Lebanon: What's the Difference?

Visitors hear the word "**service**" (pronounced *servees*) constantly.

- **Private taxi:** Only you and your group in the car. It goes directly to your destination and costs more.
- **Service taxi:** A shared car on a semi-fixed route. You pay per seat, and it stops to pick up and drop off other passengers. It's cheap, but it isn't built for luggage, schedules or privacy.

Services are fine for budget travel if you speak some Arabic and know the route. They're a poor fit for **late arrivals, first-time visitors, families with children, or anyone with bags**. Routes and departures are informal, and many are concentrated around Cola and Dora.

## How to Avoid Taxi Scams at Beirut Airport

Most drivers in Lebanon are honest. But arriving tourists and returning diaspora are easy targets. Here is how to protect yourself:

1. **Pre-book before you fly.** This is the most effective protection. Send us your flight number on WhatsApp and we track delays, so your driver is there when you land.
2. **Never accept a ride from someone who approaches you inside the terminal.** Walk to your pre-arranged driver or a clearly identified service.
3. **Agree on the price, the currency and the vehicle before you get in.** Ask whether it's a private ride or a shared one.
4. **Ask what's included.** Luggage, waiting time and tolls should be clear from the start.
5. **Know the rough distance.** Beirut Airport is in the southern suburbs, only about 15–25 minutes from Downtown or Achrafieh without traffic. Be wary of anyone who claims it's far.
6. **Carry small bills.** If you pay in cash, small denominations avoid the "no change" excuse.
7. **Screenshot your confirmation.** A written WhatsApp confirmation shows exactly what was agreed.
