## Your Real Options for Getting Around Lebanon Without Renting a Car

Renting a car is the obvious idea, but many travelers reconsider after a few days. Small-car rentals can look cheap online. Then you add insurance, fuel, parking in Hamra or Mar Mikhael, and the stress of driving through Beirut's junctions. These are the realistic alternatives:

| Option | Best for | Price level | Main drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-booked private taxi (fixed price)** | Airport, intercity trips, late nights, luggage, families | Mid, but known in advance | Needs a quick booking message |
| **Street-hail private taxi** | Spontaneous short rides | Mid, negotiated | Price depends on your bargaining |
| **"Service" (shared taxi)** | Short, local trips on known routes | Lowest per seat | Shared, indirect, shorter hours |
| **Ride-hailing apps (Uber, Bolt, Careem)** | Short city rides if you are comfortable with apps | Variable | Availability and pricing can change fast |
| **Buses and minibuses** | Budget intercity travel from Charles Helou, Dora and Cola | Lowest | Irregular timetables, little luggage space |
| **Walking** | Gemmayzeh, Mar Mikhael, Hamra, Downtown | Free | Heat, uneven pavements, distance |

## How Much Is a Taxi in Beirut? Lebanon Taxi Rates in USD

Prices in Lebanon are quoted in both **US dollars and Lebanese pounds (LBP)**. This confuses many visitors, so always confirm the currency before you get in.

Public travel guides give these general ballparks:

- **Airport to central Beirut:** often around **$15–$25** for a standard daytime taxi. A pre-booked private transfer is often quoted in the **$20–$40** range, depending on vehicle type, luggage and hotel location.
- **Short rides inside Beirut** (for example Hamra to Achrafieh): usually a few dollars up to the low teens, depending on distance, traffic and time of day.
- **Intercity trips** (Tripoli, Saida, Tyre, Zahle, Baalbek, Faraya): priced by distance and waiting time, so a fixed quote matters most here.

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport has published an official reference scale for taxis, service taxis and buses in Lebanese pounds. Because of exchange-rate and fuel-price changes, that scale is only a reference point. In practice, street prices move with the dollar rate, fuel costs and demand.

**What affects your fare:**

1. **Distance and traffic.** Dora, Cola, the Jounieh Autostrade and the Hazmieh–Baabda corridor jam at rush hour.
2. **Time of day.** Late-night and early-morning rides cost more.
3. **Luggage and passenger count.** A van costs more than a sedan.
4. **Waiting time.** This applies to multi-stop trips and flight tracking.
5. **Fuel prices.** Recent fuel levies have pushed taxi and shared-taxi prices up.

## Meter vs Fixed Price Taxi in Lebanon: Which Is Better?

Most traditional taxis in Lebanon, recognizable by their **red license plates**, do not rely on a working meter. You normally agree the price before you set off. Some companies and apps use meter-style or algorithmic pricing.

### Fixed price: how it works
You agree the total before the ride starts. It covers the whole trip, including tolls, waiting time and luggage.

**Pros**
- No surprises, no arguments at the end
- Easy to budget, and easy to compare against a quote in writing
- Better for long, multi-stop or airport trips

**Cons**
- You need to know the going rate, or get a quote from a reliable company

### Meter or per-km pricing: how it works
You pay for distance and time, so traffic can increase the total.

**Pros**
- Transparent on short, free-flowing routes

**Cons**
- A single Beirut traffic jam can double a fare
- A meter that is "broken", slow or not started is a common complaint

**Our recommendation:** Pick a fixed price for the airport and for any trip outside Beirut. For short, simple city hops, either approach works if you agree the number first. At Andrews Taxi, the price is fixed in USD or LBP before your driver arrives, and it does not change with traffic.

## "Service" vs Taxi in Lebanon: What's the Difference?

Every visitor hears the word **"servees"** (service) within a day of arriving.

- **Taxi:** a private ride for you alone. It goes directly to your destination at an agreed price.
- **Service:** a shared taxi that follows a semi-fixed route. You pay per seat, and the driver picks up and drops off other passengers along the way.

**When a service makes sense:** short trips along main streets, such as Hamra to Bliss Street or Dora to Jal el Dib, if you know the route and travel light.

**When a private taxi is better:**
- Arriving at night or after a long flight
- Traveling with children, luggage or a laptop bag
- Going to an address a service won't pass directly
- Visiting somewhere new where you don't speak Arabic

**Tip:** Tell the driver "taxi" if you want a private ride. Otherwise a driver may treat you as a shared passenger, or quote you the price of the whole car.
